Just Ends

U unfinished: not knotted; not broken; not cut... just ends termination code, Open Khipu Repository

A khipu is a record made of knotted cord, written and read with the hands. Nobody living can read the ones that survive.

619 of them have been measured and typed into a database: 54,403 cords, 110,151 knots, 16.7 kilometres of thread turned into rows.

This is an instrument that plays the rows. Each cord is a string as long as its measurement. Each knot rings where it was tied. Each cord ends the way the archive says it ended. Where the archive wrote nothing, you hear breath.

Reading is touch, and touch wears things out. Every pass of the hand loosens knots, until the main cord hangs empty and only the database is left.

Move across the cords to sound them. Press Read to pass a hand along the whole khipu.

What you are hearing

in the archivein the instrument
cord length, in centimetrespitch. The cord is treated as a string: 6600 ÷ cm gives hertz, so a 30 cm cord sounds 220 Hz. No scale is applied; the tuning is whatever the lengths are.
cord colourbrightness and ring time. Pale cotton rings long, dark cords are dull and short.
knot position along the corda partial above the cord, at the ratio (cord length ÷ knot position), like a finger resting on a string.
knot typesingle knots ping once each. A long knot of n turns is a roll of n grains. A figure-eight knot beats against itself.
knot direction, S or Zleft or right. Where direction was not recorded the knot sits in the middle and is partly noise.
terminationhow the sound ends: knotted rings out; broken tears off with a crack; cut stops clean; ravelled splits into drifting strings; doubled adds the octave below; canutito (wrapped) is muted; unfinished holds level and then just ends.
position along the main cordtime, when you press Read; left to right in the speakers.
main cord lengththe low hum underneath.
anything not recordedbreath. A cord with no measured length has no pitch at all.

What is imposed

The khipus are undeciphered and this instrument does not decode them. Turning position into time, and length into pitch, are our choices. They translate measurements, and a measurement is already somebody's decision about what counted. Pendant attachment, for example, was not treated as a sign until the 1970s, and for 26,371 cords the archive has no attachment at all.

The wear is ours too. It compresses centuries of handling into minutes so that you can watch the difference between a thing and its record: the left column of the ledger never changes, whatever happens to the cord.

What the archive does not know

  • 221 of 619 khipus have no recorded place of origin.
  • 619 have no recorded date of discovery; 617 name no finder.
  • 9,392 cords are recorded as broken, 3,000 as unfinished, 2,190 as ravelled.
  • 17,321 cords carry no knots. 12,566 knots have no recorded direction.
  • 345 of the 619 are held outside Peru, 179 of them in Germany.
